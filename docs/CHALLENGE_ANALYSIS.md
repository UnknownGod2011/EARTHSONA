# Challenge analysis

Verified against the production Space Apps site on November 14, 2026. The current Project Submission Guide and Judging & Awards Guide were published November 13, 2026.

## The Earth Information Jukebox

The official brief asks for an interface, script, or application that “pairs EIC visual frames with dynamic sonifications generated in real time.” Its objective is to produce sound that corresponds to specific NASA Earth Information Center (EIC) visual frames. The audience includes blind and low-vision users and members of the public exploring Earth science. [Official challenge details](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/the-earth-information-jukebox/)

The organizer accepts interactive tools, scripts, and curated collections of original generated audio paired with the imagery. Suggestions include translating color, brightness, shape, density, or motion into pitch, harmony, rhythm, loudness, or instrumentation. The mapping method is flexible. The brief encourages a clear mapping explanation, usable playback controls, descriptions of the visuals, and an approach that balances discernible patterns with listenable sound. Neither a 3D globe nor a machine-learning model is required. [Official challenge details](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/the-earth-information-jukebox/)

EARTHSONA pairs two ways to listen to the same underlying climate record. The frame scan reads the pixels of the selected official 2023, 2024, or 2025 NASA SVS EIC map and turns six successive stripes into tones. The timeline reads NASA GISS surface temperature anomaly values and provides comparisons in degrees Celsius relative to 1951–1980. The globe provides geographic context for the latitude bands. The image scan summarizes colors painted on the rendered map and has its own mapping and limitations. [NASA SVS #5603](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5603/) · [NASA GISTEMP v4](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/)

## Judging and participation

The rubric scores **Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance, and Presentation** from 1 to 5 at each level. EARTHSONA’s evidence for those criteria is confined to what a judge can inspect: keyboard controls and descriptions for impact, the companion frame and dataset listening modes for creativity, source-linked units and reproducible mappings for validity, EIC imagery as a direct audio input for relevance, and a short recorded demonstration for presentation once published. [Judging criteria](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/#judging-criteria)

The Submission Guide requires at least one NASA dataset or resource for a Global Award. The NASA GISS GISTEMP analysis supplies the numeric input, and NASA SVS frames are the visual input. NOAA observations contribute to NASA’s GISTEMP analysis. The judging route, deadline, exact form fields, and deliverables are covered in the [submission checklist](SUBMISSION_CHECKLIST.md). [NASA data requirement](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/#project-page-form-field-instructions)

For Global Judging, a team must respond to an official challenge, complete the form, provide a public English-language demo, and submit by the deadline. A video with other spoken languages needs English subtitles. At least one person may compete through a Local Event, and the Universal Event requires at least two. The maximum is six. All participants must appear on the team and be confirmed for the same event. Local awards and nomination to Global Judging are separate decisions. Global Nominees are scheduled for December 3, 2026, Global Finalists and Honorable Mentions for December 17, 2026, and Global Winners for January 26, 2027. [Judging & Awards Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/) · [Participant FAQs](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/participant-faqs/)

The terms require the original solution to be freely available or under an OSI-approved license, with third-party resources cited and appropriately licensed. The guide calls AI tools “Super Intelligence” and permits assistance for implementation and creativity while reserving the core solution idea for participants. It requires a description of where and how the tools were used and specifies disclosure for generated code, data, audio, images, and video. The challenge and NASA source credits are listed as attribution. [Participant terms](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/) · [SI and attribution rules](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/#project-page-form-field-instructions) · [NASA media guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)
