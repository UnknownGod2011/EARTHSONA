# Dataset research and provenance

## Candidate datasets

| Dataset and provider | Coverage, resolution and units | Access and stewardship | Product fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **GISS Surface Temperature Analysis v4, annual Land–Ocean Temperature Index (NASA GISS)** | 1880 onward through the latest complete calendar year. Eight latitude-band averages, plus broader aggregates. Units are °C departures from the corresponding 1951–1980 climate. The annual zonal CSV is a table with published latitude labels. Related monthly fields are available on a regular 2°×2° latitude/longitude grid or on the analysis equal-area grid. | Public CSV and TXT. NASA updates tables about monthly and can revise past values. The selected CSV has no missing cells in the nine used columns. This project's import rejects missing values such as blanks or `***`. Cite NASA GISS with date accessed and the uncertainty paper. [Download page](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/data_v4.html) · [CSV](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/tabledata_v4/ZonAnn.Ts+dSST.csv) · [Dataset and citation](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/) | **Selected.** Meaningful same-unit comparisons across time and regions, exact latitude-based geography, and three related NASA Earth Information Center visuals. Sparse early observations and broad aggregation constrain interpretation. |
| **Annual Greenhouse Gas Index (NOAA GML)** | Global annual series from 1979. Radiative forcing in W/m² and a dimensionless aggregate relative to 1990. A geographic coordinate reference system is unnecessary for this published global aggregate. | Public annual HTML table. Missing-value handling would need to follow the table's history and gas-specific coverage before import. Credit NOAA GML as the numerical source and NASA SVS for its depiction. [NOAA AGGI, Table 2](https://gml.noaa.gov/aggi/aggi.html) · [NASA SVS #5126 metadata](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/api/5126/) | Strong voices for distinct gas components and effective radiative forcing over time. The primary values are NOAA data. The geographic globe would give context for a global aggregate and could imply unwarranted local forcing detail. |
| **Global Mean Sea Level from Simple Gridded Sea Surface Height v1 (NASA PO.DAAC)** | 1993 onward, approximately weekly estimates from overlapping ten-day observing windows, with an optional 60-day smooth. Values in cm. The supplied scalar series integrates maps spanning 66°S to 66°N and has no local spatial resolution of its own. | Published ASCII with filename `NASA_SSH_GMSL_INDICATOR.txt` through NASA Earthdata granules. The source docs describe occasional revised historical estimates. Source flags and gaps would need validation per file. The NASA PO.DAAC citation includes the dataset DOI and access date. [Record and download](https://podaac.jpl.nasa.gov/dataset/NASA_SSH_GMSL_INDICATOR) · [EIC sea level visual #5620](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5620/) | Strong NASA/EIC match. Reveals seasonal fluctuation and long-term change. A regional comparison needs a separate, compatible sea-level product and correction choices. The indicator has no glacial-isostatic adjustment. |
| **Prediction of Worldwide Energy Resources (NASA Langley POWER)** | Global grid from 1981 for daily meteorology, near real time. `T2M` is air temperature at two meters in °C. Source grids vary by parameter. The API tutorial lists 0.5° latitude × 0.625° longitude for meteorology. Daily day boundaries can use UTC or local solar time. | Public point and regional requests. JSON, CSV and other formats. Consult API fill-value metadata per request and credit POWER and the underlying source products. Weather data archive latency is typically two to three days. [Daily API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/) · [API example](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/tutorials/service-data-request/api/) · [Archive FAQ](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/faqs/data/) | Good location-based seasonal rhythms. Fair comparison across climates would need a common analysis period and an explicit baseline for each site. A grid-cell mean can differ from local conditions, especially in complex terrain. |
| **MODIS/Terra Vegetation Indices v6.1, MOD13Q1 (NASA LP DAAC)** | Global land, nominal 250 m sinusoidal grid tiles, composite every 16 days from February 2000. NDVI and EVI are dimensionless indices. | HDF granules via the Earthdata catalog. The provider publishes quality layers to screen clouds, other invalid pixels, and fill values. NASA's public catalog links to its government works terms. Cite the dataset DOI and access date. [NASA catalog and granules](https://doi.org/10.5067/MODIS/MOD13Q1.061) · [NASA Open Data metadata](https://data.nasa.gov/dataset/modis-terra-vegetation-indices-16-day-l3-global-250m-sin-grid-v061-2eb0d) | Strong growing-season sound patterns from selected ecosystems. Needs quality masking, geographic reprojection, and matched windows before comparisons. Each composite selects a favorable observation from its 16-day window. |
| **Fire Information for Resource Management System, VIIRS and MODIS active-fire products (NASA LANCE)** | Global latitude/longitude detections at nominal 375 m for VIIRS and 1 km for MODIS. Sensor-dependent coverage starts in November 2000 for MODIS and varies for the VIIRS instruments. Records report detection time and, where available, fire radiative power in MW. | Recent area CSV via map key, historical CSV/JSON/shapefile through Earthdata Login or email. NASA publishes missing acquisition dates, and archival data can replace near-real-time detections. Credit FIRMS and the specific instrument/product. [API and missing dates](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/api/) · [Archive](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/download/) · [VIIRS fields](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/content/descriptions/FIRMS_VIIRS_Firehotspots.html) | Well suited to event rhythms and spatial audio exploration. Hotspot counts depend on overpasses, resolution and observing conditions. A burned-area or ignition interpretation would need additional data and method. |

## Selected snapshot and transformations

`public/data/gistemp-zonal-annual.csv` is the byte-for-byte NASA GISS annual zonal L-OTI download retrieved on **2026-11-14**. It covers **146 consecutive calendar years, 1880–2025**. SHA-256 `aed658e38791951f46f5d5a84554ba4f71e4fb5b569f90715429a261f40b8e21`. All 146 `Glob` values agree with the January–December `J-D` column in NASA's separate monthly table. [Zonal download](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/tabledata_v4/ZonAnn.Ts+dSST.csv) · [Global monthly table](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/tabledata_v4/GLB.Ts+dSST.csv)

The L-OTI combines estimated surface air temperature anomalies over land and sea ice with sea surface temperature anomalies over open ocean. NASA GISS creates the index using NOAA GHCN v4 and NOAA ERSST v5. The NOAA inputs feed a NASA-produced analysis. Positive values represent warmer conditions relative to that region's own 1951–1980 reference climate. Two regions with the same anomaly can have very different absolute temperatures. [GISTEMP FAQ](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/faq/) · [GISTEMP analysis](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/)

`scripts/build-climate.mjs` validates the exact header, complete rows in year order, numeric syntax, and plausible values. It selects the eight narrow bands and the published `Glob` column, keeping the values and years as supplied. The five broader regional columns remain in the raw file. It also checks that every NASA EIC still has a matching data year and the original SHA-256. `src/data/climate.json` holds the checked observations, source metadata and computed insight descriptions. Underlying precision is hundredths of a degree. The displayed values are point estimates. No additional smoothing, annual averaging, imputation, rebasing, or degree conversion is applied to the series.

The numerical values are uniform within a latitude band and span all longitudes. Labels follow NASA's rounded CSV boundaries, with more precise approximate globe edges at 23.6°, 44.4° and 64.2° north and south. `Glob` comes from NASA's global index, whose aggregation accounts for zonal area. EARTHSONA uses that supplied column directly. The globe does not show country, coastal or local climate readings. [NASA gridding and zonal averaging](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/sources/gistemp.html) · [NASA FAQ on global means and map differences](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/faq/)

Historical and regional precision varies with observation coverage, particularly early in the record. NASA publishes an uncertainty ensemble separately from this table. Values on the EIC maps and in the zonal index can differ because the maps and index aggregate gaps differently, and the illustrations are from different GISTEMP release snapshots. Sonification and chart numbers come from the pinned table. [NASA uncertainty explanation](https://www.giss.nasa.gov/pubs/abs/le07900t.html) · [NASA GISTEMP FAQ](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/faq/)

## NASA Earth Information Center visualizations

These downloaded files are unmodified NASA SVS stills from the same GISTEMP visualization series, each listed in the official EIC catalog. Each uses a Robinson projection and the 1951–1980 baseline. The stills show gridded estimates, with their original publication-specific legends and color scales. Compare numeric trends using the common-scale tracks. ## Reproduce and refresh

```sh
node scripts/build-climate.mjs
node scripts/build-climate.mjs --check
```

To review a new source release, download the official file to a candidate first. On Windows use `curl.exe` when `curl` resolves to a PowerShell alias.

```sh
curl --fail --location --output gistemp-review.csv "https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/tabledata_v4/ZonAnn.Ts+dSST.csv"
```

Review historical revisions and completeness. Replace the pinned CSV with the reviewed candidate, record the new access date in `scripts/build-climate.mjs`, regenerate, verify the values, and update the documented range, checksum, and insight figures as needed. Commit the input and generated bundle together. Keep asset originals and their hashes paired in the EIC manifest when adding a new map. Changing the displayed endpoint or adding a new dated insight requires a review of the corresponding story and app defaults.
