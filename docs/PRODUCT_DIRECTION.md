# Product direction

EARTHSONA lets a listener hear a NASA Earth Information Center frame and explore the numerical climate record behind it. A first-time visitor can play a spatial sweep of a NASA warming map or choose two tracks and listen to the same calendar years across latitude bands. The chart and data table provide quantities and units for both listening and silent use. [Official challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/the-earth-information-jukebox/) · [EIC map](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5603/)

## Concepts considered

| Direction | Creative and scientific opportunity | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Climate tracks and warming-map scan** | The red–blue color convention produces an explainable spatial sweep. NASA GISTEMP's published annual temperature anomalies add a shared baseline, 146 years of numeric evidence, eight latitude bands, and natural north–south comparisons. The linked chart makes the data readily inspectable. [NASA annual tables](https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/data_v4.html) · [NASA EIC map](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5603/) | Best combination of eligible NASA inputs, geography, quantitative traceability, and a browser implementation with no service credentials. The raster scan and numeric records need independent cues because their spatial sampling and units differ. Prior zonal climate sonifications make the interactive comparison and the companion frame pathway the distinctive work. [Sonification precedent](https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-15-00223.1) |
| **Greenhouse Symphony** | A greenhouse-gas index supports a voice for each gas family and a total-forcing motif. The EIC version offers strong thematic alignment and the NOAA table has the original annual values. [NASA SVS #5126](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5126/) · [NOAA AGGI](https://gml.noaa.gov/aggi/aggi.html) | The source visualization omits the quantitative scale. The index is a NOAA global aggregate, giving a globe little analytical purpose and supplying a less direct route for geographical selection. |
| **Ocean height and current patterns** | A NASA sea-level series and related EIC slides could distinguish the annual cycle and long-term trend. Flow frames could add directional rhythms for currents. [NASA global sea-level record](https://podaac.jpl.nasa.gov/dataset/NASA_SSH_GMSL_INDICATOR) · [NASA sea level EIC visual](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5620/) · [NASA ocean flow frames](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3912/) | The global sea-level series integrates observations between 66°S and 66°N, so regional listening requires separate geolocated values, alignment and correction choices. Currents supply a separate physical quantity and need their own defensible mapping. |
| **Fires through time** | Detections naturally generate event triggers and spatial movement. Their density and timing offer a distinct percussive experience. [NASA FIRMS API](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/api/) · [NASA FIRMS archive](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/download/) | Sensor coverage, repeat overpasses and cloud or smoke can shape the rhythm. Public findings need those caveats plus an archive or API workflow and an aligned EIC frame. |

## Selected experience

1. **Hear the frame.** Choose a NASA map for 2023, 2024, or 2025. The north-to-south sweep samples the map pixels and raises pitch for redder stripes. It reports a dimensionless image-color balance. An alternate audio source plays the global annual reading for that frame year. Each release has its own palette and processing, so use the numeric climate tracks for year-to-year comparisons. [Frame metadata](../src/data/eic.json)
2. **Inspect the climate record.** Choose a primary zone and a comparison zone, then listen to one annual step per beat. The same pitch scale applies across regions and time. Year, selected tracks and data values agree across controls, the plot and the keyboard-accessible data table. [Mapping](SONIFICATION_METHODOLOGY.md)
3. **Find an observable difference.** Open a preset, compare its tracks, and reproduce the finding from the source rows. The discovery text identifies the period and scope before interpreting the pattern. [Analysis and caveats](DISCOVERED_INSIGHTS.md)

The Blue Marble globe gives broad latitude bands geographic context. The Earth picture is a fixed July 2004 composite and the band colors encode the selected year's annual anomaly. The demo can show that distinction in one view by rotating the globe with a fixed selected year. [Basemap provenance](../public/earth/README.md) · [Annual data provenance](DATASET_RESEARCH.md)
